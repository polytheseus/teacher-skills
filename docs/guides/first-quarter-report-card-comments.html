First quarter report card comments: 147 examples for the first report of the year, and a free builder

The first report card of the year is the one families read most closely, and the one teachers have the least to go on. Six to ten weeks in, it's tempting to write "Ava has had a good start" and move on. Here's a pattern that says what you've actually seen so far and sets one goal while there's most of the year left to reach it, with 6 settling-in lines, comments in 12 areas, 15 goals with what you and the family can do, 50 short comments, 22 rewrites and a free builder that writes and checks each comment. Want them on paper? Download every line as a printable PDF (free, no sign-up).

Updated 10 November 2026 · First quarter, first trimester or first term · K–6, with UK wording · About a 7-minute read

The short version: a first quarter comment does four things. Says how the student has settled in (routines, confidence, getting to know the class). Names one or two things that have gone well, specifically. Sets one goal for next quarter, the part families act on. Says who'll do what: one line for you, one for home. Leave out end-of-year wording copied from last June ("a great year"), "too early to tell", and verdicts like "below grade level" that a family should hear from you in person first.

The four-part pattern

PartWhat it doesExample
1. Settling inAnswers the first thing families wonder about in a new class"Noah was quiet in our first weeks and is starting to share more, especially in small groups."
2. What's gone wellOne or two specific things, so they know you see their child"He reads every day in our reading time and can retell what happened in a story."
3. One goalThe line families actually do something with"Next quarter, Noah will work on sharing ideas in class discussions more often."
4. Who does whatMakes the goal real, with one thing for you and one for home"To help with this, I'll give notice of a question so Noah can think of an answer first. At home, you could ask Noah to tell you one thing learned at school each day."

Short on space? Keep parts 2 and 3. A real moment from these weeks ("retold The Little Red Hen to the class, with all the voices") is worth adding whenever you have one.

What makes the first report different

  • You're describing a start, not a finish. Write about what the student does now and what comes next. The year-end comment sums up; this one sets direction.
  • Many standards are end-of-year goals. On many standards-based report cards, a mark of "developing" or "not yet" in the first quarter is normal, because the standard is what students should reach by June. Say so if your report card works that way, so families don't read it as failing.
  • It often comes with a conference. In many US schools the first report card goes home around fall parent-teacher conferences. Anything serious (a big gap, a behavior concern, talk of extra support) belongs in that conversation first, not as a surprise in writing.
  • The goal has time to work. A goal set now has three quarters to show progress, and the next report can say how it went. That's what makes the goal line worth writing carefully.

Six ways first quarter comments go wrong

  • "Too early to tell." Families read the first report hoping to learn how their child is doing in a new class. After six weeks you've seen something: how they start the day, how they read, who they work with. Say that.
  • End-of-year lines copied forward. "Ava has had a great year" or "ready for second grade" in November tells the family the comment came from a bank. The builder's checker catches these.
  • "Settle down." It reads as a telling-off. Name the habit and the goal: "waiting for a turn to speak in group discussions".
  • "Below grade level" in writing first. If it's a real concern, say what the student can do now and the next step, and talk to the family before they read the report. Retention never appears for the first time in a comment.
  • Praise that fits anyone. "A good start" and "a pleasure to have in class" are true of most of the room. Say what made the start good.
  • Labels. "Lazy", "immature", "can't", "refuses". Six weeks into a new class, a lot of what looks like an attitude is a child still learning the routines. Describe what happens and what comes next.

What to look at before you write

Six weeks of notes are thinner than a year's, so gather the little there is before you start the batch:

  • Beginning-of-year checks: reading levels, letter-sound or number checks, a first writing sample. These show where each student started, so the next report can show the change.
  • One piece of work per student from the last two weeks, for the "what's gone well" line.
  • Your own notes on settling in: who still needs reminders for routines, who has found friends, who has said little.
  • A list of anyone you need to talk to first. If a comment would surprise the family, call or meet before the report goes home.

Common questions

What do you write on a first quarter report card?

Say how the student has settled in, one or two specific things that have gone well, and one goal for next quarter, with a line on what you and the family can do to help. For example: "Noah was quiet in our first weeks and is starting to share more, especially in small groups. He reads every day in our reading time and can retell what happened in a story. Next quarter, Noah will work on sharing ideas in class discussions more often."

What are some short first quarter report card comments?

Short ones that still say something: "Has settled quickly into our class and knows our routines well." "Listens carefully to instructions and gets started straight away." "Is willing to have a go at writing and adds more detail each week." "Next quarter, will work on recalling number facts quickly." Should I write "below grade level" on a first quarter report card?

Be careful. On many report cards the standards are what students should reach by the end of the year, so "not yet" in the first quarter is normal. If there's a real concern, write what the student can do now and the next step, and talk to the family before the report goes home. Never raise holding a child back for the first time in a written comment.

What should I write for a student who hasn't settled in yet?

Describe where they are and the direction it's going: "is still learning some of our routines and is more comfortable with them each week", then one small goal, such as getting started on tasks after instructions, with what you'll do to help. Avoid "needs to settle down", which reads as a telling-off.

How is a first quarter comment different from an end-of-year comment?

The end-of-year comment sums up and looks to the next grade. The first quarter comment describes a start and sets one goal for this year. That's why copied lines like "has had a great year" or "ready for third grade" stand out on a November report card.

Can I use ChatGPT to write first quarter comments?

Yes, within your school's AI policy, if you leave out names and private details. Give it a code for each student, your notes and the goal, tell it this is the first report of the year, and ask it to avoid stock phrases. Then read every comment: generic praise is exactly what these tools produce when the notes are thin.
