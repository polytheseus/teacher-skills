Australian report comments: Semester 2 examples for Foundation to Year 6, and a free builder

Semester 2 reports go home at the end of Term 4, and most of them are written in November. Here are 82 comments in Australian English for English, maths and learning habits at three levels, how to make each comment agree with the A–E grade beside it, the reporting rules behind it, end-of-year closings, and a free builder that writes and checks comments for the first year of school, whatever your state calls it.

Updated 29 October 2026 · Australia, Foundation to Year 6 · About a 9-minute read

The short version: a good Semester 2 comment names one or two specific strengths from this year, one next step for next year and one small thing to do at home or over the holidays, in plain Australian English. It should agree with the grade: a C means working at the expected standard, so say that, rather than "excellent" (which reads like an A) or "needs to try harder" (which reads like a D). Write about the child's own progress, never compared with classmates.

Make the comment agree with the grade

The most common complaint from Australian families isn't a harsh comment. It's a comment that doesn't match the letter next to it. Here is what each grade means on the NSW common grade scale (other states use similar wording), and how a comment at that level sounds.

GradeNSW descriptorWhat the comment should sayExample (Year 4 maths)
AExtensive knowledge and understanding of the content; can readily apply itApplies skills to new or harder problems; explain how"Jack applies his multiplication facts to multi-step problems and explains why his strategy works."
BThorough knowledge and understanding; a high level of competenceSecure and accurate, with a stretch next step"Jack solves multiplication problems accurately. A next step is choosing the most efficient strategy."
CSound knowledge and understanding of the main areas of contentWorking at the expected standard. Say so plainly"Jack is working at the expected standard in maths. He solves multiplication problems using arrays and known facts."
DBasic knowledge and understanding; achieved a limited levelWhat he can do, the one gap that matters most, and the help in place"Jack adds and subtracts confidently and is now learning his 3, 4 and 6 times tables, with daily practice in class."
EElementary knowledge and understanding in few areas of the contentA real strength, a clear plan, and an invitation to talk"Jack counts by 2s, 5s and 10s and is building his multiplication facts in a small group. I'd welcome a chat about how we can practise together."

A D or E comment should never be the first time a family hears there's a problem. If the grade will surprise them, call or meet before reports go home. For more wording at that level, see comments for struggling students.

Australian wording, not American

Most comment banks online are American. Before you paste one into your report system, swap these:

USAustralia
first grade, third gradeYear 1, Year 3
mathmaths
quarter, report cardterm or semester, report
practicing, organized, color, behavior, recognizepractising (verb), organised, colour, behaviour, recognise
recess, summer vacationrecess and lunch, the summer holidays
period, grade (punctuation)full stop
middle schoolhigh school (from Year 7 in every state)

Leave syllabus codes and achievement-standard language out of the comment ("MAE-RWN-01", "working towards the achievement standard in AC9M2N01"). The grade already carries that; the comment is where a parent finds out what it means for their child.

Semester 2 closing sentences

The last sentence of an end-of-year report looks ahead to next year and the summer holidays. Pick one that's true for the child:

  • Ruby should be proud of everything she has achieved this year. Enjoy a restful summer break, and I look forward to hearing about Year 3.
  • Thank you for your support at home this year. Ruby is ready for the step up to Year 2.
  • It has been a pleasure watching Jack's confidence grow this year. Have a safe and happy holiday.
  • Jack has worked hard all year and is well prepared for Year 5. Over the holidays, reading together for ten minutes a day will keep those skills fresh.
  • Thank you for a wonderful year. I know Aanya will bring the same curiosity to high school.
  • Aanya has made strong progress since Term 1. Keep up the home reading over the break, and enjoy the summer.
  • Next year, Jack can keep building on this by practising times tables in the car or at dinner, a few minutes at a time.
  • Jack has been a kind and reliable member of our class. I wish him every success in Year 4.
  • Well done on a big year, Ruby. Have a happy and restful break with your family.
  • Thank you for working with me this year. Aanya finishes the year confident and ready for what comes next.

Common questions

How often do Australian schools have to send reports?

At least twice a year, under section 59 of the Australian Education Regulations 2023. Most schools report at the end of Semester 1 and Semester 2. The report must be easy to understand and, for Years 1 to 10, give an A–E grade (or equivalent five-point scale) for each subject.

Do Foundation or Kindergarten students get A to E grades?

The Commonwealth A–E rule covers Years 1 to 10. NSW says A–E grades aren't required in Early Stage 1, where teacher comments can describe progress. Victoria requires a five-point rating from Foundation. Follow your state and sector's policy.

What does a C grade mean?

On the NSW common grade scale, a sound knowledge and understanding of the main areas of content: the expected standard. Say so in the comment, because many families read C as average or poor.

How long should a report comment be?

Follow your school's limit; many report systems allow somewhere between 400 and 1,000 characters per comment. Four to six short sentences is usually enough.

Can I use ChatGPT to write report comments?

Only within your school or department's AI policy, with codes instead of names and no sensitive details. Read and edit every comment before it goes into the report system.
