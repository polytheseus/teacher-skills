Sub plan template: a free builder, emergency sub plans and the sub folder checklist

Sub plans get written at 6 a.m. by a teacher with a fever, and they're run by someone who has never met the class, may not teach the subject, and has five minutes to read them. Here's a one-page format that works under those conditions, a free builder that prints it and catches the usual mistakes, emergency plans that work on any day of the year, and what to keep in the sub folder.

Updated 27 October 2026 · US sub plans and UK cover work · About a 7-minute read

The short version: one page per day. At the top: who to call for help, how to take attendance without your login, where the seating plan is, and the arrival and dismissal routines. Then each block with a time, exactly what students do, where the materials are, and what to collect. Plan practice or review of work the class already knows, give a paper fallback for anything on a screen, add one early-finisher task, and ask for a note at the end. The sub plan builder below lays it out and flags what's missing.

The one-page sub plan template

Substitutes and cover staff look for the same things in the same order. This layout puts them where they'll be found:

SUB PLAN · Tuesday 3 November
Ms Rivera · Grade 4 · Room 12

IF YOU NEED HELP: Office ext. 200, or Mr Okafor next door (Room 13)
ATTENDANCE: Paper class list on the clipboard by the door. Send it to the office by 8:45.
SEATING PLAN: Red folder on my desk, first page
ARRIVAL: Students unpack, put folders in the basket and start the morning work on their desks.
BATHROOM: One at a time with the hall pass. Not in the first or last 10 minutes.
HELPFUL STUDENTS: A.K. and R.M. know where everything is.
EARLY FINISHERS: Silent reading book, or a puzzle sheet from the green tray.

SCHEDULE
8:30-8:50  Morning work
   Do: Spiral review sheet, then check answers with a partner (answer key in the folder).
   Collect: sheets in the basket
8:50-9:50  Reading
   Do: Read "The Stray" (pp. 42-47) together, then answer Q1-6 in full sentences.
   Materials: readers on the shelf, question sheets in the blue tray
   Collect: question sheets, names on top
...
END OF DAY: Car riders at 3:05 to the gym, walkers at 3:10 with the duty teacher.
PLEASE LEAVE ME A NOTE: who was absent, who was helpful, any problems, and anything you didn't get to.

Four rules make it work:

  • Every block has an output. "Answer Q1-6 in full sentences" tells the substitute what done looks like, and the collected sheets tell you what actually happened.
  • Practice, not new content. A non-specialist introducing a new concept tends to create misconceptions you'll spend a week undoing. Use review of the last two or three lessons.
  • No logins. The substitute can't open your Google Drive, your slides or your gradebook. Print it, or give a link students open with their own accounts.
  • Behaviour is half the plan. Arrival, bathroom, dismissal and who to call matter more than the lesson. Most sub-day problems happen at transitions and with early finishers.

Emergency sub plans that work any day

Emergency plans are for the morning you can't write anything. They have to work at any point in the year, with any unit, and without you. Write two or three days of them, make the copies now, and put a note on top: "Can be used at any point in the year." Ideas that hold up:

AgeActivities that need no context
K–2 / Reception–Year 2Read-aloud picture book with a draw-and-label response ("draw your favourite part and write one sentence"); a number or phonics review sheet from earlier in the year; a "my weekend" or "if I could fly" writing page with sentence starters; a directed drawing; a familiar song or movement break.
Grades 3–5 / Years 3–6A short story with comprehension questions graded from easy to stretching; a mixed math review sheet (all skills already taught) with an answer key; a persuasive or opinion paragraph on a fun prompt with a model; a logic puzzle or word search for early finishers.
Middle school / Years 7–9A one-page article or extract with questions; a retrieval quiz mixing topics from the whole year; a data or graph interpretation task for science or geography; a "design a…" task with a success checklist.
High school / Years 10–13An exam-style question set on earlier topics with a mark scheme for students to self-mark; a reading with a written response; key-term glossary or flashcard making for the current course; a past-paper section timed as practice.

Whatever you choose: a product students hand in, an answer key for the substitute, and an early-finisher task. Replace each plan as soon as it's used, or you'll find an empty folder next time.

What to keep in the sub folder

  • The one-page plan for each emergency day, on top, with copies already made and clipped to it.
  • Class information sheet: routines, timetable, duties, who to call, fire and lockdown procedures (or where they're posted).
  • Seating plans, with a photo or initials only if your school prefers.
  • A paper class list for attendance, and how to send it to the office.
  • Answer keys and model answers.
  • Early-finisher work for any lesson.
  • Medical needs the substitute must act on, kept the way your school's policy says (often a separate sheet): what to do and who to call, not diagnoses.
  • A "note for my sub" page with questions: who was absent, who was helpful, any problems, what didn't get done.
  • Where things are: the hall pass, spare pencils, the board remote, the key.

Student privacy: a sub plan is left on a desk, photocopied and sometimes filed. Write what to do, not why: "J.T. may take a movement break: allowed" rather than a diagnosis, and "only the named adults on the office list may collect E.F." rather than family details. IEP, 504 and EHCP information, safeguarding concerns and medical history stay in your school's secure systems, shared with a substitute the way your school's policy says. The builder flags these words, and they should never go into an AI chat.

UK: cover work for supply teachers and cover supervisors

The same format works for cover, with a few differences. Cover is often run by a cover supervisor, who supervises pre-set work rather than teaching, or by a supply teacher who may not be a specialist, so write the lesson as independent work from the start. Say where the register is and how to take it if they can't log in to the school's system, name the on-call or behaviour system ("send a reliable student to reception for on-call"), and give a single lesson per page with the class, period and room at the top. Exercise books are the easiest thing to collect: "write the date and title, answer in full sentences in your book" means the work comes back to you with no extra paper.

Using ChatGPT, Gemini or Claude to write sub plans

AI tools are quick at turning "8B, food chains revision, textbook pp. 112-115, 50 mins" into a full plan and worksheet. Check your school's AI policy first, and leave out student names and anything sensitive. Then paste this:

Write a sub plan / cover lesson for [Grade 4 reading / Year 8 science], [50] minutes. A substitute who doesn't teach this subject will run it.
The class has just learnt: [food chains, producers and consumers].
Resources: [textbook Activate 1, pp. 112-115; printed worksheets; no student devices].
Use this format: CLASS, TOPIC, YOU NEED (with exact locations), ON ARRIVAL, then timed blocks from 0 minutes with an expected output for each, an EXTENSION for early finishers, a COLLECT step, and ANSWERS on a separate page.
Rules: review only, no new content. No step may need my login or accounts. If any step uses a screen, add a paper fallback. Then write the student worksheet itself, with questions from easy to stretching.
If something is missing that you'd need to guess, ask me instead.

Read it as the substitute would: could you run it with five minutes' reading and no help? Then make the copies and put them where the plan says.

The note from your substitute

Leave a short form so you get useful information back, not just "they were fine":

Thank you for covering today!
Absent: ______
Helpful students: ______
Any problems (who, when, what you did): ______
Work not finished: ______
Anything I should know for next time: ______

Common questions

How do I write a sub plan?

One page per day: who to call for help, attendance without your login, the seating plan, arrival and dismissal routines, then each block with a time, exactly what students do, where the materials are and what to collect. Add an early-finisher task and a note request. Plan review of work the class already knows, not new content.

What should be in emergency sub plans?

Lessons that work any day without your input: a short text with questions, a mixed review sheet, a writing task with a model, and a puzzle or problem-solving sheet, with copies made. Keep them in a labelled folder with the class information sheet, seating plan, class list and the school's emergency procedures.

How many days of emergency sub plans should I have?

Many teachers keep two or three days ready, and some schools require a set number, so check your handbook. Replace them when they're used, and refresh the class information sheet when routines or seating change.

What do I write if I'm sick in the morning?

If the emergency folder is ready: which day to use and anything that changed. If not: a review task on this week's work, with pages and questions named and the work collected at the end. Send it to the office or cover contact as early as you can.

Can I use ChatGPT to write sub plans?

Within your school's AI policy, with no student names or sensitive details. Give it the lesson facts, ask for a fixed one-page format with times, materials and a collect step, then check every line.
