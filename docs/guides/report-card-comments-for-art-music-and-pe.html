Report card comments for art, music and PE: examples by skill and level, and a free comment builder

If you teach art, music or PE, you might write 300, 500 or 700 report card comments, often in a box that holds two sentences, for children you see once a week. So it's tempting to paste "Maya enjoys art" 400 times. Families notice. A specials comment can be short and still say something true: one thing the child does well in your lessons, one real skill, and one next step. Below: wording for four areas in each subject at three levels, participation strengths, habit next steps, home tips, 18 rewrites of phrases to avoid, and a free builder that writes and checks each comment in your character limit.

Updated 30 October 2026 · Grades K–8, with UK wording (art and design, PE kit, term) · About a 7-minute read

The short version: a good art, music or PE comment has three parts. A participation strength ("takes good care of the instruments", "is kind and encouraging to classmates"). One real skill at the student's level ("keeps a steady beat and copies rhythms accurately on percussion instruments"). One next step ("Next, Leo is working on reading simple rhythms from written notes"). That's about 300 characters. If there's room, add a home tip. Leave out talent labels ("not sporty", "a natural"), anything about weight or body size, fitness test scores, medical details and comparisons with classmates. Before you write: four things that are different in specials

  • You have hundreds of students and very little space. Decide your three parts once, then vary the skill and next step by unit and level. That's what the builder does; the participation lines are shared across all three subjects so you only learn one set.
  • Skills are taught, not born. "Tone deaf", "not sporty", "can't draw" and "two left feet" tell a family the skill can't be learned. "Talented" and "a natural" do the same thing from the other side. Name the skill and the next step instead. The US national standards describe all three subjects as learnable skills: the National Core Arts Standards are built around creating, performing or presenting, responding and connecting, and SHAPE America's 2024 National PE Standards cover motor skills, movement and fitness knowledge, social skills, and personal skills and choosing to be active.
  • PE comments touch on bodies. Never comment on weight, size, BMI or how out of breath a child gets, and keep fitness test scores (FitnessGram, PACER, the beep test) out of the written comment: they're personal, and many schools share them separately. Comment on effort, skills and the student's own progress.
  • Participation problems need a plan, not a complaint. If a student often has no kit or sits out, say what they are working on ("bringing PE kit to every lesson") and talk to the family. If the reason is medical or personal, leave it out of the report entirely.

Habit next steps

A second, smaller next step about how the student works. Use one at most, and only if it's the main thing that would help.

  • Sam is also working on bringing gym clothes and sneakers to every lesson. (PE)
  • Sam is also working on listening while others are speaking or performing.
  • Sam is also working on helping to tidy up materials at the end of the lesson. (art)
  • Sam is also working on practicing their instrument for a few minutes most days. (music)
  • Sam is also working on waiting patiently for their turn.
  • Sam is also working on staying focused for the whole lesson.
  • Sam is also working on starting tasks straight after the instructions.
  • Sam is also working on staying calm and positive whether their team wins or loses. (PE)

Common questions

How do you write a report card comment for art, music or PE?

Name one participation strength (effort, care with materials or equipment, kindness to classmates), then one real skill in the subject at the level the student is working at, then one next step. Add a home tip if there is room. Two to four short sentences fit most specials comment boxes.

How long should a specials report card comment be?

Usually shorter than a homeroom comment: many report systems give art, music and PE teachers 250 to 500 characters. Strength, skill and next step fit in about 300 characters; drop the home tip or closing line first if you are over.

What should I write for a student who doesn't participate in PE?

Say what the student does do, then one next step and what would help, for example 'is working on bringing PE kit to every lesson'. If the student sits out for health, medical or family reasons, leave it out of the written report and talk to the family instead.

Should I comment on fitness test results like FitnessGram or the beep test?

Usually not in the written comment. Fitness scores are personal, and many schools share them separately. Describe effort and the student's own progress, such as 'keeps going a little longer each time in running activities'.

Is it OK to call a student talented or gifted in art or music?

It's kind, but it suggests the skill is a gift rather than practice, and it says little. Name what the student does instead: 'chooses colors to show mood', 'holds a part in a round', 'finds space to receive a pass'.

Can I use ChatGPT to write art, music and PE report card comments?

Yes, within your school's AI policy, if you use a code or first initial instead of names and leave out medical, family and support-plan details. Give it the subject, the unit, the level and one note per student, ask for short comments with one next step, and read every comment before it goes home.
