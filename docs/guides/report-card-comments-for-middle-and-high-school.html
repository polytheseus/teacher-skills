Report card comments for middle and high school: examples for any subject, and a free comment builder

A middle or high school teacher might have 150 students across five or six sections, a 500-character box, and a few evenings to fill it. So the comment bank comes out: "A pleasure to have in class." "Could do better if he applied himself." Students read these now, and so do their families, and neither can do anything with them. A good secondary comment is short and says three things: one thing the student does well in your class, where they are in a real skill, and one next step they can act on in the next unit. Below: wording for seven areas that work in any subject at three levels, learning strengths, habit next steps, careful wording for missing work and students at risk of failing, 18 rewrites of phrases to avoid, and a free builder that writes and checks each comment in your character limit.

The short version: a good middle or high school comment has three parts. A learning strength ("acts on feedback to improve her work", "asks thoughtful questions when something is unclear"). One real skill at the student's level, named for your subject or unit ("In our work on cells and microscopy, Jordan plans and carries out practical work carefully"). One next step the student can act on ("Next, Jordan is working on explaining what his results show and why"). If there's a problem, add one plain sentence about it and where to get help. Leave out grades and percentages (they're already on the report), predictions ("will fail"), discipline records, home circumstances, medical and support-plan details, comparisons with classmates, and vague lines like "could reach his full potential". Before you write: five things that are different in middle and high school

  • The student is your main reader. From about grade 6, students read their own reports, and many open them before their families do. Write a next step they can act on themselves ("planning each answer before writing"), not a verdict on them ("needs to try harder").
  • The grade is already there. Don't spend your characters repeating "62%" or "a C". Use the comment for what's behind the grade and the one thing that would move it.
  • You teach a subject, not a class of 25. The seven areas below work in any subject: knowledge, problem solving, written work, discussion, assessments, practical work and independent study. Pick the one or two that matter most for this student, and add the unit to make it yours.
  • Problems need a plan, not a complaint. Missing work, a failing grade or gaps after absences belong in the comment, said once and plainly, with a deadline and where to get help. Discipline incidents, home circumstances and medical details don't: they go through your school's systems and a conversation.
  • Keep the door open. In England, a pupil's annual report must include brief particulars of achievements, "highlighting strengths and developmental needs", and tell parents how to arrange a discussion about the report (DfE guidance). US districts set their own rules, but the same shape works: strength, next step, and an invitation to get in touch when there's a concern.

Habit next steps

A second, smaller next step about how the student works. Use one at most, and only if it's the main thing that would help.

  • Sam is also working on handing in every assignment by its deadline.
  • Sam is also working on staying focused for the whole lesson.
  • Sam is also working on keeping their phone put away during lessons.
  • Sam is also working on taking full notes in every lesson.
  • Sam is also working on asking for help as soon as something is unclear.
  • Sam is also working on reviewing their notes for ten minutes after each lesson.
  • Sam is also working on starting work promptly at the beginning of each lesson.
  • Sam is also working on reading each question carefully before answering.

Missing work and students at risk of failing

When there's a real problem, say it once, plainly, with what will help. The builder adds the sentence below, then the next step, then where to get help (your extra-help session, lab or catch-up club), and ends with "Please get in touch if you would like to talk about how we can support [Name]." Families should never learn from the end-of-year report that a student was failing all year: if a student is at risk, most schools expect a call or email before the report goes home, and the comment confirms the plan.

The "effort" line is the one to use most carefully. Only use it when you've seen the student do better work, and pair it with a specific next step and a real strength.

UK secondary reports: target grades, GCSE and A level

Switch the builder to UK for "term", "below target", "past papers", "practising" and UK spelling. Most secondary reports in England show a working-at grade and a target grade next to the comment, so the comment doesn't need to repeat them: "is currently working below her target grade, and there is still time to close the gap" says what the numbers mean. Keep exam-board language (AO1, AO3, assessment objectives) out of the comment; say what the student does instead ("backs up each point with a quotation and explains it").

If your school only gives you comment codes

Some report systems only let you pick from a fixed list of coded comments. If yours has a short free-text box as well, use it for the next step alone: "Next, Jordan is working on explaining what his results show and why." Set the limit to 150 and the builder keeps the strength, skill and next step and drops the rest. If there's no free text at all, put the next step in your next email home or on the student's work.

Writing to the student instead

Some schools ask teachers to write comments to the student ("You write clear answers…"). Pick one voice and keep it all the way through: the checker flags comments that mix "you" with the student's name. The builder writes about the student by name; to switch, change the name to "You" and the verbs that follow ("You write", "You are working on").

Common questions

How do you write a report card comment for a high school student?

Name one learning strength that's true for the student, then one real skill in your subject at the level they're working at (name the unit if you can), then one next step they can act on in the next unit. If there's a concern such as missing work, add one plain sentence with a deadline and where to get help. Three or four short sentences fit most comment boxes.

What should I write for a student who is failing?

Say it once, plainly, with what will help: for example 'Sam is currently at risk of not passing Algebra 1, and there is still time to turn this around.' Then give one specific next step, where to get help (an extra-help session, math lab or catch-up club) and an invitation to get in touch. Lead with a strength that's true, and contact the family before the report goes home rather than letting the comment be the first they hear.

How do you write about missing assignments on a report card?

Name it without blame and give a deadline: 'Jordan has some missing work this quarter, and handing it in by November 20 will give a fuller picture of what Jordan can do.' Add where to get help and one next step, such as handing in every assignment by its deadline.

Should I put the student's grade or percentage in the comment?

Usually not. The grade is already on the report, so the comment is better spent on what's behind it and the one thing that would move it. Predictions such as 'will fail' or 'won't get into college' are best left out too.

Is 'could do better' or 'not working to potential' a good comment?

It's very common, but the student can't act on it. Say what would make the difference instead: 'Next, Mia is working on adding a specific piece of evidence to support each point.'

Can I use ChatGPT to write middle and high school report card comments?

Yes, within your school's AI policy, if you use a code or first initial instead of names and leave out grades, medical, family, discipline and support-plan details. Give it the course, the unit, a level and one note per student, ask for short comments with one next step, and read every comment before it goes home.
