Report comments rarely take long one at a time. What hurts is 25 or 30 of them, often in three subjects each, written in the evenings in the same fortnight as everything else. The fix is mostly planning: know how many hours it really is, spread it out, and stop rewriting the same sentence from scratch.

1. Plan the writing

Time yourself writing three comments first, because a guess is usually too low. Then fill this in. It counts the hours, spreads the class evenly over the days you can work, and keeps the last day or two before the deadline for reading them all through. If it doesn't fit, it tells you how much time a day you'd need, or how many days.

3. The 5-step routine

  1. Plan the days with the planner above, and put them in your calendar.
  2. Make a notes grid before you write a word. One row per child: a strength you've seen, one next step, one moment you noticed, and any mark or level the comment must match. Use initials or class numbers, not full names, if it lives on a shared drive or goes near an AI tool. Ten seconds a child here saves minutes later.
  3. Write the hardest first in each session, while you're fresh. If a comment needs to say something difficult, check the family has already heard it from you (see step 12 of the check).
  4. Use one pattern: a real strength with evidence, one next step and what you're doing about it, one small thing to try at home, and a warm close. For example:

    Maya explains how she solved a word problem, drawing a bar model to show her thinking. Next step: check answers with the inverse operation; we're practising this at the start of each lesson. At home, ask her to "prove it" when she tells you an answer. It's been a pleasure seeing her confidence grow this term.

  5. Batch by subject, then check. Writing all the math comments in one sitting keeps them consistent with your marks; then run the check below on the whole set, not one at a time.

4. The 12-point check before you submit

Most of these can be checked in a minute per child. The builders' checkers do points 5 to 9 automatically: paste a comment into any of them.

  1. Right name, every time. Search the document for each name you used. Copying a good comment and missing one name is the most common report card mistake there is.
  2. Right pronoun, and the same one all the way through.
  3. No placeholders left: search for "[", "Name", "XX" and "he/she".
  4. Words match the mark. "Exceeding expectations" next to a 2 out of 4 confuses families and invites questions.
  5. No labels ("lazy", "disruptive", "a reluctant reader"): describe what you see instead.
  6. No comparisons with classmates, siblings or "the rest of the class".
  7. No diagnoses, support-plan details or guesses at feelings. Those belong in a meeting and in your school's secure systems.
  8. No jargon a family wouldn't use: "decoding", "fronted adverbials", standard codes.
  9. Under your system's character limit, and straight quotes if your report system turns curly ones into "?".
  10. One next step, not five, and it says what you're doing about it.
  11. Read three aloud in a row. If they sound the same, vary the openings and the moments, not the substance.
  12. Nothing the family hears for the first time. A real concern about learning, behavior or wellbeing should come in a conversation first, with the report confirming it.

Questions

How long does it take to write report card comments?

Time yourself on three, because it varies a lot. As an example, 25 students with one 8-minute comment each is about 3 hours 20 minutes of writing, plus proofreading; with three subject comments each it's about 10 hours. A notes grid made before you start, and a builder or comment bank for the wording, usually cut that a lot.

Can I use ChatGPT to write report card comments?

Yes, if your school allows it and you keep student data out: use codes instead of names, share only the teaching notes the comment needs (never diagnoses, support plans or family details), and read and edit every comment before it goes in. Our free guide shows a safe prompt and how to put the names back afterwards.

What should a report card comment include?

A specific strength with evidence, one next step with what you're doing about it, one small thing the family can do at home, and a warm close. Keep it to what you've seen, in plain words, with no labels, comparisons or diagnoses.

Are these comment builders free?

Yes. Every builder and checker on this page is free and runs in your browser, so nothing you type is sent or stored. The printable comment bank is a paid download for teachers who want every line in one editable file.
